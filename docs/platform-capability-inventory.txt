##### Finding businesses to sell to
This part of the product is meant to fill a list of real local businesses, show you which ones have a bad or missing website, build each one a free demo site, and email them a pitch. There are TWO separate front doors that do overlapping versions of this and do not share their results. "Prospects" (in the always-visible sidebar) is a saved, growing list: one orange button, "Scrape the web (Claude)", goes out and finds real businesses across US metros and files them into a pool you keep forever; each row has a one-click "Build & send". "Find clients" (buried under More > Prospecting) is a throwaway search box: you type a trade and a town, it finds businesses, actually opens each one's website and grades it 10-100 with a Weak / Dated / Already solid verdict, and you press "Build their site" one at a time — but the results vanish when you leave the page. The grading engine is the real product here (src/lib/garvis/siteAudit.ts): it only reports things it genuinely saw on the page (no HTTPS, no mobile setup, no contact form, thin content, an old copyright year, missing page title) and refuses to invent a score for a site it could not load. Nothing on the Prospects list is graded at all — that page only knows "has a website" or "doesn't". None of it works out of the box: finding businesses needs either an Anthropic key or a Google Places key set in Supabase secrets, and sending needs four more things (APP_ORIGIN, a Resend key, a from-address, a mailing address, and the outbound switch flipped on). Every business search costs real money per search, and one press of the big orange button on Prospects can fire up to 120 paid AI searches.

##### Building a website for a prospect
This is the money-making loop of the whole product: find a local business that has a bad website (or none), automatically build them a better one, email it to them as a link they can open with no login, and let them raise their hand or pay. There are THREE separate doors that all build the same thing — the "Build & send" button on Prospects (the good one: it reads the prospect's real site, writes a custom page, emails the pitch), the "Build" button on Find clients (paste or search a URL), and the Preview Engine page (paste a blob of JSON — the technical door). What gets built is stored in your account as a "preview site" with its own secret web link like /preview-site/joes-roofing-k3x9p2; that link is live and public the moment it is created, hosted inside your own app, no extra setup. The business owner opening that link sees their new site, an honest "here's what's wrong with your current site" report, a bar to claim it, and a Stripe buy button. Turning the demo into a REAL website on a real address ("Go Live") and pointing the client's own domain at it are both built and wired to Netlify, but neither will work until a Netlify token is set on the server, and there is no screen in the app to set it. The single most important hidden setting is APP_ORIGIN: until it is set on the server, the one-click "Build & send" button refuses outright, because the demo link it would email would be broken. Everything else in this cluster degrades honestly rather than faking — with no AI key you still get a site, just a plain templated one instead of a custom-designed one.

##### Emailing prospects and clients
This part of the product is the machine that writes, approves, and actually mails email — cold pitches to businesses you want as clients, and warm mail to people already in your book (replies, invoices, reports, reminders, newsletters). Everything funnels through ONE door: no button anywhere sends an email directly. Every button writes a draft into a table and drops a card into the Queue, and only pressing Approve on that card in the Queue causes an email to leave (src/lib/garvis/execution.ts:114 → supabase/functions/send-email/index.ts). The one exception is bulk sends and a few clock-driven rules, where you approve a whole batch once and a background clock mails them out a few at a time under your daily cap (supabase/functions/standing-worker/index.ts:247-380). Before any of it works, five things must be true, and four of them are set by a developer on the server, not by you in the app: an email provider key (RESEND_API_KEY), a public web address for demo links (APP_ORIGIN), a shared clock secret (WORKER_SECRET), the clock itself switched on ("Arm" on the Health page), and then — in the app — your from-address, your legal mailing address, and the outbound master switch flipped on in Settings. With any one missing, the buttons still look alive, the cards still land in the Queue, and the send silently refuses. The honest verdict for this operator: the plumbing is genuinely good and the safety rails are real, but almost nothing in this cluster is usable out of the box, and — worst of all — when a send is refused, the Queue tells you "Edge Function returned a non-2xx status code" instead of the actual reason the server produced, which is exactly why it feels like "anytime I try to do anything it doesn't work." There are also three separate doors for cold-pitching one business, two separate doors for mailing a contact segment, and a monthly client-report email with no button at all.

##### Social posting and content channels
This part of the product is for running short-video channels and social accounts end to end: it writes a script with real sources, makes the pictures, reads the narration aloud, renders a vertical video, and then posts it to your real TikTok / YouTube / Instagram / Facebook / LinkedIn accounts — but only after you personally press Approve in the Queue. There is exactly ONE way a post ever reaches a real account: something creates a post row plus a "Publish post" card in your Queue (Sidebar > Queue); you approve it; the app calls one server function (supabase/functions/social-publish/index.ts) which re-checks everything and hands the post to Ayrshare, an outside service that holds the actual connections to your social accounts. Nothing in this app logs into TikTok or Instagram directly. So the single thing that makes all of this real is: you sign up at Ayrshare, link your accounts THERE, and paste that Ayrshare API key into Sidebar > More > Account > Settings > Connections. Until that key exists, every studio still lets you write, draft, and render — and then the publish step stops with "No social account connected." The second thing that matters is money: Ayrshare's free tier posts IMAGES ONLY, and this whole channel engine produces mp4 videos, so the flagship path needs a paid Ayrshare plan or it will fail at the last step. The third thing is server keys you do not control from inside the app (OPENAI_API_KEY for the pictures and voice, SHOTSTACK_API_KEY for the video render) — without them "Produce" stops with a named message telling you which key is missing. Everything that works unattended (posting an approved post while your laptop is closed, pulling back view counts, the daily auto-draft) depends on a background clock that must be armed once from Sidebar > More > Automation & status > Health.

##### Running client work and getting paid
This is the part of the app that is supposed to carry a client from "they said yes" all the way to "they paid me and got a report." In practice it is four separate desks that never talk to each other. Desk one, "Clients" (the Client book), is a notepad: who your client is, what you agreed to do, and a tick-list of things you still need from them. Desk two, "Client revenue," is a completely different list of completely different people: the ones you charge a monthly or one-time fee, with your Stripe payment links and your MRR total. Desk three, "Money," is plain invoices you write, email (after approving them), and mark paid by hand. Desk four, "Fleet," is an alarm board that tells you a client's monthly report is overdue or a request is going stale — but there is no page anywhere in the app that lets you write a client report or open a change request, so those alarms can never be cleared. The one genuinely automatic path is narrow: a prospect clicking "Make it mine" on their demo site goes through a checkout that records the sale and can publish their site by itself. Every other way you get paid — including the "copy payment link" button on the Client revenue page — leaves you doing the bookkeeping by hand, because that copied link carries no identifier Stripe can hand back. Nothing charges a card from inside the app; Stripe's own hosted pages do that, and only if you have pasted your payment links in first. And the polite late-payment chaser, which is the most valuable thing here, is switched off out of the box in three different ways at once.

##### What runs on a clock without the operator
This is the part of the product that is supposed to work while you are asleep: a database timer (pg_cron) that pokes 13 different background workers on a schedule — every 5 minutes, every 15 minutes, hourly, nightly, weekly, monthly. Those workers watch web pages, hunt for new businesses, draft follow-up emails and invoice chases, fire per-customer reminders, send appointment reminders, pull social numbers, and check the system is still alive. Almost none of them ever SEND anything: they put drafts in your Queue and wait for you to press approve. The whole thing is off until someone runs one database command ("arm the heartbeat") with a password that lives in the Supabase dashboard, not in this app — and until that happens every one of these features fails completely silently. There are three status pages that tell you whether it is on: Health (the master switch and the secrets list), Working for you (what the clock is doing right now), and Fleet (only the client problems that need judgment). The single most important line in the whole cluster is the small clock banner that says "Clock ticking — last tick N min ago" versus "The clock has never ticked"; if it says the latter, every promise on every other page in this cluster is not happening. Two live phone/web surfaces — online booking and missed-call text-back — run on outside webhooks rather than the clock, so they can work even when the clock is dead, but only after Twilio and a public booking link are configured.

##### The AI layer — asking for things in plain language
This is the part of the product where you type or say a sentence instead of hunting for a button. There are three doors into it and they are NOT the same thing. (1) The little bubble in the bottom-right corner of every page — "Say what you want to do" — which is mostly a very good, instant, free shortcut-finder that needs no AI at all. (2) "Home" in the sidebar, which is a full chat with Garvis that can answer, plan, open a studio beside the conversation, or go do work. (3) "Orchestrate" (hidden under More > Build), where you say a whole paragraph — "found the agency, research the market, write the plan, watch these grant pages" — and get back a numbered plan you approve before anything runs. Behind those sit two memory rooms (Memory = your uploaded documents on one tab, Garvis's own record of goals/beliefs/decisions on the other) and three older rooms — Missions, Mission Control, Brain, Mind — that are still live at their web addresses but were deliberately taken out of the sidebar. The single most important fact for this operator: almost everything that answers in words needs a server-side AI key set on the deployment, and there is currently NO visible indicator anywhere in a normal production build telling you whether that key is set. The honest-failure messages are mostly good ("The brain isn't answering — is an AI key set?"), but Orchestrate's failure toast leaks raw plumbing text, and the corner bubble tells you to "go ask Garvis on Home" — which, with no key, is also dead. Worse, there are TWO separate key settings that fix DIFFERENT halves of the product, and the one the operator can actually reach (Settings) only fixes about half.

##### Building apps (the original product)
This half of the product is the original "describe an app and it writes it for you" factory, and it still works better than almost anything else in the repo — but it has been pushed to the back of the house. You log in and land on the agency Home (/garvis/command), not here; every door into app-building sits behind the sidebar's "More" chevron under a section called "Apps" (src/lib/navConfig.ts, src/components/layout/AppShell.tsx lines 150-215). What lives here: a list of your apps, a "describe it in a sentence" builder that first offers you three different visual directions, an importer that pulls in an app you already have from GitHub or a zip, a background build queue called Autopilot, and the workspace itself — a chat pane on the left, your code in the middle, a live running preview on the right. The chat is the real product: you type "add a settings page", it reads your files, writes them, and type-checks its own work. The workspace then piles fifteen more buttons on top of that — Brain, Map, Next, Check, Ideas, Theme, Database, Data, Secrets, Terminal, Autopilot, Cloud — and roughly a third of them cannot work on a deployed copy of this app at all, because they are still gated on a local-developer switch (VITE_AI_DIRECT) that is off in production. Those buttons do not hide or explain themselves; they throw a message like "Map generation currently requires direct mode (edge mirror coming)" (src/lib/aiClient.ts lines 157, 197, 231, 245, 265, 838). That is almost certainly a large part of "anytime I try to do anything I feel like it doesn't work" — the operator is clicking real-looking buttons that were never finished for the mode they are running in. It overlaps the agency half rather than conflicting with it: the agency side (worlds, Preview Engine, workshops) hands ideas INTO this builder via /new?idea= and /new?from=world, and every publish from here routes through the agency's Queue approval spine, so the two halves are wired together at the seams even though they read as two different products.

##### Configuration — what must be true before anything works
This is the plumbing layer: the handful of screens where you tell the app who you are, hand it the keys to the outside services it uses (email, maps, video, e-signature, social, payments), and switch on the clock that makes it do things while you sleep. Almost nothing else in the product works until this cluster is satisfied, and almost none of it can be done from inside the app — roughly twenty secret keys have to be typed into the Supabase dashboard by someone comfortable with a command line, and a database script has to be pasted in before several of these screens even load. The app is unusually honest about this once you find the right page: /garvis/health lists which keys are set and which background jobs are scheduled, and /garvis/setup gives you a 5-item essentials checklist. But those two screens live behind the sidebar's "More" fold, nothing on the Home page points you at them, and they disagree with each other about what "ready" means. The single biggest trap is the clock: you can press "Arm the heartbeat" on Health, watch 13 of 13 jobs turn green, and still have a system where nothing ever runs — because the secret you typed has to exactly match a WORKER_SECRET you set separately in Supabase, and when it doesn't, the jobs fire and get rejected silently. The second biggest is that the Health page only checks 27 of the 73 functions that actually exist, so "all deployed" is not the same as "everything is there." Everything in this cluster is genuinely built and wired to real tables and real functions — the gap is that a non-technical operator cannot complete any of it alone.
