# Where the drift makes money — the applications research (live web, 2026-09-01)

*Sixth interrogation round, and the second one done with live web access rather than generated
reasoning: five researchers (the StumbleUpon lineage, discovery commerce, the 2024–26 new-social
landscape, a 20-vertical corpus sweep, and B2B/licensing/data), 341 searches and fetches, a
referee ranking every application by expected money for this founder. The founder's question,
verbatim: "any other ideas that I could make money on this… is it a new social media, is it for
shopping, I know you mentioned podcasts… Is it a website like StumbleUpon? Dig into this."
Every number below carries a source in the session record; the load-bearing ones are cited here.*

---

## 1. Direct answers

**A StumbleUpon-style website? No as a business, yes as a demo.** StumbleUpon was real money
once — $30M revenue in 2012, ~75,000 advertisers buying "stumbles" at $0.05–0.25 per visitor,
eBay paid $75M (2007), founders bought it back for $29M (2009). It died of four things in this
order: Pinterest's *scannable grid* took its referral niche in six months (scanning beat
stumbling); its iframe mechanic broke when sites blocked framing; it never found a mobile-native
gesture ("swipe to stumble" was a relabeled button); and feeds absorbed the time-killing job. The
lineage's revealed willingness to pay today is ~zero: Cloudhiker earned ~€1,000 lifetime after
10M page-views; Wander hit 370 HN points and $0; stumbleupon.cc's $3.99/mo paywall met a revolt;
Digg 2.0 (Rose + Ohanian + True Ventures) opened Jan 2026 and pulled its app Mar 2026 after bots
gamed the discovery. The one profitable "serendipity" business is Are.na — $125K MRR after 15
years, and it's a *curation tool*, not a stumble button. **Verdict:** a small-web "Stumble
field" seeded from Kagi Small Web / Wander's curated sites is a weekend build, a near-certain
Hacker News hit, and the best screen-recording the content engine will ever get. Marketing,
never revenue. Generic-web discovery has no payer; the drift wins on *bounded, judgeable*
corpora, not the open web.

**Shopping? Not as the business.** Gesture-led shopping is 0-for-7 in a decade — Mallzee,
Grabble, Kwoller, Stylect, Amazon Scout, Amazon Inspire, and Flip ($230M raised, shut Aug 2025).
Every survivor owns supply, sellers, or ad-intent, never the gesture. Affiliate math for a
scrub-browse session is ~$78–290 per 1,000 sessions, so $10K/month needs 30–130K sessions a
month against 3–6% D30 — a treadmill only the content engine makes survivable. The one shopping
shape that fits the drift's own laws (hunt + doneness + bounded) is a **Q4 gift-hunt world** —
a seasonal content play, not a P&L.

**A new social network? No.** Every discovery-first social launched 2024–26 stalled or died
(Digg twice, Bluesky MAU down ~52% from peak); what retained was a private friends graph plus
passive presence (Yope, Retro, Airbuds), which a taste-scrub mechanic does not supply. What *did*
grow was curation inside a bounded domain tool — Letterboxd ~5M → 26M in about a year. Take
exactly two social affordances — the shareable trail as an export, and one communal daily world
per vertical — and skip profiles, follows, and feeds until a single-player world shows D30 ≥25%.

**Podcasts (the previous world #2)? Demoted.** The corpus is legally fine (Podcast Index, public
RSS — the earlier licensing worry stands only for redistribution posture), but podcasts fail the
drift's *own* constraints: speech needs a ~30s clip to judge, not a <400ms transient; 71K
episodes/day is a firehose, not a 30–500 world; the only payer is Apple's 50% on a tiny
paid-subscription base; and Spotify shipped Clips in May 2026. Scored 15/25 — 13th of 20.

## 2. The new winner: Sample Drift

Loops and one-shots for music producers scored 24/25 — the best fit in the entire sweep, on
every axis the drift's own constraints define:

- **Scent:** the truest sub-400ms verdict that exists — a kick hits or it doesn't. Audio *is*
  the feedback, so this is also the world where the web version (no haptics) loses least.
- **Ritual match:** producers already "audition" hundreds of sounds per session by clicking
  through lists with play buttons — Splice's browse UI is exactly the surface the drift
  replaces, and one detent per sample maps 1:1 to the audition gesture.
- **Payers:** Splice — >$100M revenue, ~600K subscribers at $12.99–39.99/mo, affiliate
  $11.20 per paid sub plus recurring renewals (60-day cookie); Loopcloud 15% recurring.
- **Corpus, legally free:** Freesound — 714,671 sounds, 72% CC0, ~129 new/day (naturally
  bounded), free API key with negotiable commercial terms. V1 needs nobody's permission.
- **Buyers who just proved appetite:** Splice bought Spitfire Audio for a reported ~$50M in
  April 2025; Loopcloud/Loopmasters, Native Instruments, Output are the other rooms.
- **Distribution fit:** producer-niche short-form ("made a beat from random samples") is a
  proven content lane for the 50-account engine, and every session is audible screen-recording.
- **The signal:** "skipped 40 trap hi-hats in two seconds" is taste data no sample store has.
- **Risk:** Splice has no public catalog API — V1 is Freesound + label-permissioned packs;
  Splice's own catalog comes via partnership, not scraping.

Honest bands: $60–300K/yr revenue; exit $1–5M (acqui-hire / discovery-layer purchase). Smaller
than the podcast-to-Spotify fantasy, but real, legal, buildable this month on the audio-detent
engine already scaffolded, and with four named buyers instead of one.

## 4. B2B, honestly

Product-discovery SaaS is a ~$700M+ ARR category (Algolia ~$230M ARR, Constructor ~$65M at a
$550M valuation, Coveo $142.5M FY2026, Bloomreach ~$220M) — but every dollar is *ranking and
search* sold as conversion lift to enterprise merchandisers; nobody in it has ever bought a
browse *interaction*. Shopify paid developers $1.3B last year; discovery apps price $29–699/mo;
the free incumbent (Shopify Search & Discovery) sits at 2.7★ — an opening, but a small one, and
web storefronts get the haptic-less version. Attention data as a standalone product: no
(Adelaide is sub-Series-A after four years; Mallzee Insights died). Gesture licensing à la
SwiftKey ($250M) required 300M-device distribution first. **Single B2B wedge:** "Drift Browse"
for visual Shopify catalogs, $29/$99/$299, with a steer-away heatmap the free app can't show —
300 stores ≈ $500K ARR → 3–4× profit exit to a roll-up. Fallback, not headline.

## 5. What changes in the plan

- **World #2 is Sample Drift, not podcasts.** Podcasts go in the drawer with the reasons above.
  The audio-detent engine, hook-cutting, and world spine carry over unchanged.
- **Buyer slate reorders:** Splice / Loopcloud / Native Instruments / Output become the first
  rooms; Match stays as the second room (gesture value); Pinterest/Snap drop to warm.
- **Shopify Drift Browse is the named fallback** if Scout misses the D30 gate at month 6.
- **The small-web Stumble field ships as a free demo** when the naming write-up goes out.
- **This week:** pull a Freesound API key; apply to the Splice and Loopcloud affiliate programs;
  stand up a 200-sound CC0 daily world on the existing engine; screen-record five sessions with
  audio for producer-niche accounts; get it onto 20 producers' phones. Kill metric, set now:
  **D7 ≥40% among those 20**, or Sample Drift joins podcasts in the drawer and the Shopify
  wedge moves up.

## 6. Founder-fit correction (same day)

The operator's objection, verbatim: "It seems a bit ridiculous to get into music industry
randomly." Correct, and it exposes a flaw in §2–3: the ranking scored *corpus fit* (scent,
payers, free data, buyers) and ignored *founder fit*. Entering a vertical the founder doesn't
live in is a classic solo-founder death — no dogfooding, no taste, no buyer relationships, and
the 50-account engine doesn't reach producers.

The sweep's real lesson is the **pattern**, not the vertical: the drift monetizes best on
creator-tool subscriptions reached by affiliate, over bounded, judgeable, legally-free corpora
**the founder uses daily**. Applied to the founder's own world — a pipeline producing short-form
video at volume — the corpus is the **creator asset hunt**: sound effects, music beds, b-roll,
fonts, templates. Same scent virtues (Freesound's 72% CC0 corpus is mostly SFX; Pexels/Pixabay
video; Google Fonts), same affiliate class (Adobe Stock $43/sub verified; Epidemic Sound,
Artlist, Envato Elements programs exist — rates to verify), and buyers in the founder's own
operating world: Canva, Adobe, CapCut, Envato, Epidemic Sound. The producer sample-library
variant becomes a later adjacency if the mechanic proves. New standing rule in the plan:
**never enter a vertical you don't use daily — every world must have you as user #1.**
