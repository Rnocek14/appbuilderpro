# Reality Check 11 — Comprehension Attack

*Phase 4 adversarial pass. Lens: does a normal, smart, non-technical human understand this
product without a manual? Sources audited: `_constitution.md` (§2 esp.), 01, 03, 11, 12 (every
visible label), operating-model §3. Format: sixteen named attacks (A1–A16) woven through the
six mandated analyses, ending in the five most dangerous failures ranked, each with its
smallest fix. Every attack cites the doc/section it strikes and stages the confused user's
inner monologue verbatim.*

---

## 1. Mandated analysis 1 — the first-hour simulation

Meet Dana: runs a two-person cleaning business, smart, fluent in Gmail, Canva, Square. Not a
developer. Hour one, honestly narrated.

**Minute 0–2.** Empty Home: one line — *"Say anything — a question, a business, a thing to
build — and I'll make it a world."* (12 §1.4). Dana's first wrong model forms in the first
sentence: **"a world."** *"A world? Is this a game?"* She has no referent. She types the
safest thing she knows: "help me write a price increase email."

**Minute 2–5 — A1: Enter mints entities.** The router detects no home for this and — per
constitution §7 and 11 §10.7 — *silently materializes a world*. The URL changes; that is "the
whole ceremony" (12 §4.2). Dana now has an orb on Home named something like "price increase
email." Inner monologue: *"I asked for an email. Why is there a bubble? How do I delete it?"*
There is no delete (P12: "there is no delete because there is only dormancy and archive," 01).
The zero-ceremony doctrine (P4) is priced for the operator who already understands worlds; for
the hour-one user it converts every stray thought into a permanent object she cannot remove.
The docs celebrate this ("asking permission to be curious kills wonder," 01 P4); Dana
experiences it as a product that litters and refuses to clean up. **Nothing in 12's W1 empty
state teaches what a world is, that it is permanent, or that permanence is safe.**

**Minute 5–15.** She finishes the email. It "stages at the gate" (W3 language). *"What gate?"*
She hunts for a Send button; instead the Pulse top-right ticks to `[1·0·0·✓]` (12 §0.3).
**A14: the Pulse is four unlabeled counts and a checkmark.** *"One-zero-zero-check... is that
a version number?"* The four segments are waiting · blocked · needs-you · clock health — and
"clock health" corresponds to no concept Dana possesses (the Clock is a hidden substrate,
const §2). Worse, W1 §1.3 reveals that "waiting" and "needs-you" are *different counts*
(Kessler's site review is needs-you, not waiting) — a distinction no user will ever
reconstruct: to Dana, everything waiting needs her.

**Minute 15–25.** She finds the Queue (it opened when she clicked the Pulse). Her draft is
there with `(Approve a)(Edit e)(Decline d)(Hold h)`. This part works — approving her own email
is comprehensible. But the item carries "score 8.6 vs your outreach criteria v3 — why ▸" (W2).
*"My criteria? I never wrote criteria. What's v3? There were two before?"* The versioned
rubric is one of the product's best mastery ideas (P1) and one of its worst first
impressions: it asserts a shared history that does not exist yet.

**Minute 25–40 — A11: the mystery dial.** Back on the new world's screen she notices four
dots, `●○○○`, unlabeled by design (03 §5.1: "the word 'posture' appears nowhere; the dial
needs no title"). She clicks the third dot. The room *reorders* — staged items shuffle, the
side content changes emphasis (03 §3.5). *"I broke it. How do I get it back?"* She clicks
around until it looks familiar. The dial is deliberately never named, never explained, and is
also moved *for* her by the router based on her words (03 §5.2) — so the room sometimes
rearranges itself when she didn't touch anything. An unlabeled control with invisible
semantics that also self-actuates is not minimalism; it is a haunted house.

**Minute 40–55.** She types "what should I charge for deep cleans?" — the router lands her in
an Explore surface (const §7); the right margin starts growing a node map (W4). *"What is it
drawing?"* The map-delta chip says "+2 concepts · 1 source." She never asked for a map. It is
the product's most distinctive surface and it arrives without one sentence of introduction —
W4 §4.2 allots exactly one dim line ("the map grows as you talk"), shown once, ever.

**Minute 55–60.** She goes back to Home. Two orbs now. The Brief has nothing to say (honest —
const §4). Her hour ends with the wrong model fully formed: *"It's a chatbot that keeps
making bubbles."* Every signature system — vitals, Playbook, automations, missions, lenses —
requires days of accumulated rows before it renders anything at all (P9 forbids theater, so
the surfaces are honestly empty). **The product's first hour cannot exhibit the product.**
The docs never confront this: 09/12 specify empty states that "say the words," but an honest
empty state is still an empty hour.

## 3. Mandated analysis 3 — the invisible-architecture audit

The constitution promises Genome/Capability/Spine/Situation/Line never appear (§2, §15), and
12 §11 check 4 audits the *strings*. The strings are indeed clean. The **concepts** are not.

**A8 — genome layering leaks whole, wearing the word "setup."** To answer the adoption
proposal the constitution mandates ("your client setup improved: adopt the new booking
flow?" — const §11), the user must hold this model: my worlds derive from a shared layer;
that layer just changed; adopting rewrites parts of *this* world but my local edits are
diffs that survive; declining forks me from future improvements. That *is* genome layering —
inheritance, override, propagation — renamed but not removed. W5 makes the anatomy visible:
"based on your proven client setup, used ×9, refined 4 times ▸ (**unfolds the layers, each
✎**)." W3a's scrub preview demands even more: the user must referee what "travels" to the
shared layer vs. what "stays here" — the patterns-travel-data-doesn't doctrine (P11) as an
interactive judgment. Inner monologue at the adopt gate: *"If I say yes, does it change the
site we already built for Harbor? Will my custom invoice wording survive? If I say no, am I
off the upgrade train forever?"* None of those three questions is answered by any wireframe
surface. **The word was hidden; the homework wasn't.**

**A15 — "scope" is Line-architecture leaking as a foot-gun.** The Bar's standing scope can be
set to a world you are *not in* via the switcher's "address, don't go" long-press (11 §7).
From then on, typed intent lands in a world other than the one on screen. This directly
contradicts 12 §11 check 2 ("the context header and the Bar's scope chip name the same scope
on every frame") — the two documents fight, and the user loses either way: either the feature
exists and the chip/header disagree, or the concept "scope ≠ location" must be taught, which
is teaching the Line.

**A10 — the Spine leaks as the knowledge gate.** "Nothing steers behavior without having
passed this human" (W2) is a beautiful invariant, but the Queue item it produces asks the
user to approve a *proposition* — «Tuesday sends beat Thursday» — with no statement of
consequence. *"Approve... that a fact is true? What happens if I do? What is it going to DO
with this?"* The gate's meaning (this sentence will now steer drafting everywhere in this
world) lives in 17, not on the item. Users will either rubber-stamp (gate becomes theater) or
freeze (gate becomes a chore) — both defeat the Spine the concept exists to protect.

**A16 — the ontology's absences read as missing features.** P7's proudest refusals — no
Missions page, no Automations app, no Memory room (11 §5: "their absence *is* the IA") —
will be experienced by normal users as holes: *"Where do I see all my automations?"* The
answer (a lens chip named "Running" at Home) exists but is undiscoverable as an *answer to
that question*, because the user is asking a where-question and the product's reply is a
category correction ("automations aren't a place"). The user is not wrong to ask where; the
product is wrong to require the philosophy before the answer.

Also leaked-in-concept: **earned autonomy per action-class** (the Queue's dial offers, W2)
requires the user to grasp that trust is granted *per template of action per world* — a
matrix no surface draws; and **the two truths of the Builder preview** (fast preview vs full
runtime, W8) is honest engineering that still demands users understand there are two
renderers, one of which lies slightly.

## 4. Mandated analysis 4 — the six-products test

One operator, one day: morning ops → Jane's client world → the apparel bench → the inbox
automation → the bee-hive rabbit hole → the Builder.

**What transfers (credit where due).** The Bar's position, the Pulse, the Queue with
`a/e/d/h`, the Face-vitals tap-through, Continue, `✎`/`⟲` chips, commit rails, "History ·
Where you left off." The grammar (03 §8) genuinely holds for the *chrome*. The five-questions
table (03 §8) is real.

**Reflexes that do NOT transfer — the itemized bill:**

1. **What Enter does** (A5). At Home the Bar routes and *creates* ("make it a world"); in the
   bee-hive world the Bar *is the conversation* (W4 placeholder: "ask, wonder, branch…"); in
   a session it is a counsel side-channel ("more like #3, but…", W6A; "tighten the ask…",
   W6B). Same pixel, three speech acts. The interpretation chip is the only tell, and it is
   fine print above an input that muscle memory autopilots through. *"Sometimes typing goes
   somewhere, sometimes it makes things, sometimes it just... talks."*
2. **The name of recurring work** (A12): "Automations" at Jane's, "Routines" at Mom's (03
   §7.2), "the routine" inside the Support Inbox world, edited in a "**Recipes**" area (03
   §7.5) via "the **recipe studio**" (W10) — four words, one concept, chosen per world. The
   skin system, built to make worlds feel distinct, is the single largest manufacturer of the
   six-products feel the constitution forbids (§15).
3. **The meaning of silence.** Everywhere else, quiet = healthy (P3). In the curiosity world,
   the Face *cannot* warn (03 §2.4) — a different idiom for the same visual quiet. And in
   automations, silence is an *alarm* ("silence is loud," const §8). Quiet means healthy,
   structural, or broken depending on genome — the user must know which room they're in to
   read the same absence.
4. **"Branch"** means a git branch in the Builder (W8 "branches: main · hero-B"), a map fork
   in Explore (W4 "( branch here )"), and nothing anywhere else. Different mechanics, same
   word, same product, same day.
5. **Score/critique reflexes** exist only on benches with criteria packs; the inbox world and
   view-areas have none. Tournament, blind-score, kill-with-why: apparel-bench-only dialect.
6. **The three history doors** (A7): "what happened?" is answered by **History** (session
   ledger), **Activity** (mission step recorder, W9), or **Run history** (automation trace,
   W10) — chosen by *which kind of actor did the thing*, a taxonomy the user doesn't have.
   Plus the Queue's **Auto-ran** tab as a fourth record surface. Four doors, one question.
7. **Vitals reflex breaks in the curiosity world**: the learned gesture "tap the named health
   signal for its rows" finds no vitals there at all — momentum and beacons instead.

Verdict: it feels like **one shell containing three products** — an ops console (Brief/
Queue/Desk), a craft suite (benches, dialects per craft), and a research notebook (Explore's
map, visually and behaviorally alien to everything else) — with the terminology-skin system
actively working to make sibling worlds feel like different vendors.

## 5. Mandated analysis 5 — the explanation test

After a week, the three most likely one-sentence explanations to a friend:

1. *"It's like ChatGPT, but it remembers everything and turns each thing you're doing into a
   little bubble."* — the hour-one model, fossilized. Misses gates, automations, craft;
   formed because the first week's surfaces are honestly empty (A1, §1 above).
2. *"It's an AI that runs your business and drafts everything, and you just approve stuff in
   an inbox."* — the Queue-reduction. The user who lives in operate mode (const §14 layer 1)
   never meets the benches; the mastery thesis (P1) is invisible to them.
3. *"Honestly it's hard to explain — it's a project thing where everything is a 'world' and
   there's a map that grows when you talk to it? You have to see it."* — the accurate one,
   and the most damning: a week in, the product's own best user cannot compress it, because
   the product never hands them the sentence. No surface ever states what the whole thing is.

## 6. Mandated analysis 6 — abstractions deletable from the user's head, zero capability lost

1. **The posture dial as a visible control** (03 §5). By its own spec it gates nothing and
   only re-lights (§5.4). Keep implicit posture from words; delete the four mystery dots.
   Capability lost: zero — every item is reachable in every posture by design.
2. **The momentum-is-not-a-vital distinction** (03 §2.5.5). A doctrinal subtlety no user will
   ever perceive; fold the trend mark into the Face's presentation and stop legislating it.
3. **Terminology skins for invariants** (const §2): Routine/Automation, Studio/Workshop
   variance. Delete the variance, keep domain vocabulary for *content* ("the wall," "drops").
4. **"Beacon"** as a headword — keep the park-a-question function, call it what users will
   call it (a pinned question with your guess held).
5. **The waiting/needs-you split in the Pulse** (12 §0.3) — collapse to one "for you" count;
   blocked and clock keep their segments.
6. **The layer anatomy in Proposals** (W5 "unfolds the layers") — users need *provenance*
   ("proven across 9 clients") and *consequence* (a diff), never the stack itself.
7. **"World" as taught vocabulary at first contact** (W1 §1.4) — the empty-state line can
   promise outcomes ("I'll set it up and keep it") without the cosmology; let "world" be
   learned from the switcher once there are three of them.
8. **The History/Activity/Run-history trinity** (A7) — one user-facing word ("Activity")
   everywhere an actor's record opens; the internal taxonomy stays internal.

## 7. The five most dangerous comprehension failures, ranked

**1. A8 — Setup-layer adoption demands invisible architecture.** (const §11; W5; W3a)
The user is asked binding questions ("adopt the new booking flow?", "promote to your client
setup?") whose safe answers require the genome-layer model the product promised to hide.
Wrong answers propagate configuration across every derived world.
*Smallest fix — hide harder:* render every adoption/promotion as a **concrete per-world
before/after diff** ("Harbor Dental would gain: a booking step — see it") with provenance as
social proof only ("proven in 9 worlds"); never show layers, never ask about generalization —
the scrub preview becomes a system guarantee stated in one line, not a review task.

**2. A1 + A2 — Entities appear from nothing and vanish from view, and there is no delete.**
(const §4, §7; 01 P3/P12; W1)
Hour-one users mint junk worlds they cannot remove; week-one users watch healthy worlds
disappear from the Field and conclude data loss. Both are the same failure: object permanence
is real in storage and violated on screen, and nobody told the user which layer they're
looking at.
*Smallest fix — teach-by-doing:* the first silent birth gets one inline sentence ("kept —
nothing to save, nothing to lose; ask for it anytime"); the Field always ends with the census
row ("61 quiet · everything ▸") even at 5 worlds, so absence always has a visible floor.

**3. A3 + A4 — Four unnamed AI actors, one of whom sounds human.** (12 §0.2; W6A; W9; const §6)
The counsel has deliberately no name, changes personality per world (service-minded here,
socratic there), and shares the stage with "the worker" ("hand off," "10 more like #3
**overnight**"), the router, and routines. Users will perceive one moody entity — or worse,
believe "worker/overnight/hand off" means human contractors, a wrong model with trust and
billing consequences.
*Smallest fix — merge and rename:* one assistant identity everywhere (stance is tone, never
persona); driver stamps read "you · assistant · scheduled"; "Hand off" becomes "keep going
without me."

**4. A12 + A13 — The skin system and copy leaks manufacture the six-products feel.**
(const §2; 03 §7.2/§7.5; W2; W3; W8)
Routine/Automation/Recipes for one concept; "at the gate," "rung 2," "this class," "mount
back... area + verbs" as visible strings. Each is small; together they are why the product
reads as glued-together.
*Smallest fix — delete + rename:* strike skin variance for cross-world invariants (one word:
Automation); a string audit that bans gate/rung/class/mount/verbs from rendered copy —
"waiting for your OK," "follow-up 2 of 3," "emails like this one."

**5. A10 — Approving knowledge is an act with invisible consequences.** (W2; W3a; 17 via W2)
The lesson-gate item asks the user to approve a sentence without saying what approval does,
so the product's central learning gate degrades into rubber-stamping or dread.
*Smallest fix — teach in the ask:* every gate item states its consequence as its first line
("If you approve, drafts in this world will prefer Tuesday sends — used by: follow-ups ·
undo anytime"), making the gate self-explaining forever.
