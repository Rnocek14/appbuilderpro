# One to One Billion — the growth strategy (2026-08-17)

The build is done and deep-tested; this is the OTHER plan — how a one-operator machine becomes a
billion-dollar company. Same discipline as the build plan: three acts, each with pre-registered,
evidence-gated exits. Nothing scales on vibes; every act earns the next with measured rows.

## The arithmetic (so the target is a number, not a mood)

A ~$1B valuation at ordinary 2026 SaaS multiples (8–12× ARR; agentic leaders trade higher) means
**$80–120M ARR**. The reference points from our own competitive research: Lovable $13.3B on massive
traction; **Base44 ≈ $150M ARR in 11 months with a tiny team** — the existence proof that small,
focused, vertically integrated wins in this category. The bet is NOT "out-build the funded
horizontals." It is the shape they don't have: **the vertical operator platform** — build the
business's software AND run the business (outreach, mail, invoicing, follow-ups, deploys) through
one approval queue on the operator's own keys. Builders churn when the app ships; an operating
system retains. Retention is the whole game at these multiples.

## Act I — Prove the machine can SELL (now → ~3 months) · $0 → ~$15k MRR

Deploy is triggered (mode=full, heartbeat armed). Then the machine runs its own first business:

- **Design partner #1: the real-estate vertical** (the farm, postcards with per-household QR
  attribution, MLS sync, drips — all built). One real agent's marketing runs end-to-end on it.
- **The agency-of-one motion**: the prospect hunter finds local businesses with broken web
  presence, builds real demo sites, pitches through the approval queue. Close at $500–2,000/mo
  per client for site + marketing automation (client_subscriptions/billing already wired).
- **Everything measured from day one**: channel results, prediction calibration, review-gate
  ledger. The four-week clocks (SW8.1 routing, review-gate flip, Field checkpoint) all run here.

**Exit gates (pre-registered):** ≥10 paying clients; ≥$10k MRR; measured channel table showing at
least one channel with act-grade evidence (MIN_SAMPLE + ACT_RESPONSES); churn known, not guessed.
**Kill signal:** 3 months of full-throttle hunting with <3 closes → the pitch or the vertical is
wrong; fix positioning before spending anything on scale.

## Act II — Productize the operator (months 3–12) · → $1–3M ARR

Two SKUs, both already load-bearing in the codebase:

1. **"Garvis for Real Estate" (self-serve SaaS)**: $99–299/mo, credits metering + Stripe plans
   already built. Distribution: the same prospect hunter now sells the SaaS; every deployed
   client site is a footer-credited demo (the preview-site → claim loop exists); the vertical's
   own community channels. The free tier runs the cheap model tier by design — CAC stays low.
2. **Agency-in-a-box**: white-label FableForge+Garvis for freelancers/small agencies (the client
   worlds, packages, reports, and billing surfaces exist). Per-seat + revenue share.

Team stays near-zero: the review stack, verify suites, and nightly probes ARE the engineering
org; hire only where agents can't sign (sales conversations, compliance counsel).

**Exit gates:** $1M ARR run-rate; **NRR ≥ 110%** (the operator-platform thesis lives or dies on
expansion revenue); self-serve activation ≥ 40% of signups reaching an approved first send;
gross margin ≥ 80% with model costs fully loaded. **Kill signal:** NRR < 90% at 6 months of
cohort data → we built a tool, not an operating system; stop scaling spend and fix retention.

## Act III — The platform (years 2–4) · → $100M+ ARR

- **The vertical playbook, repeated**: each new vertical = a channel kit + compliance pack +
  template pack on the same spine (real estate → home services → local health → boutique
  fitness…). One vertical per quarter once the playbook is written down from Act II.
- **The data flywheel** (the real moat at scale): thousands of businesses' MEASURED channel
  outcomes → privacy-respecting benchmarks ("postcards at 8%+ turnover farms convert at X") →
  the cross-venture engine recommending plays across the whole customer base, not one operator's
  portfolio. Nobody else has honest, attributed, cross-channel outcome rows — the honesty spine
  is what makes this data trustworthy enough to sell.
- **Outcome pricing**: per-lead / per-close upsells on top of subscriptions — the QR-to-household
  attribution and payment reconciliation needed to bill on outcomes already exist.
- **Marketplace**: user-built kits/templates with revenue share.

**Gates:** each new vertical must clear Act-I-style evidence before the next opens; benchmark
products ship only from aggregates with real sample discipline (MIN_SAMPLE inherited, always).

## The moat, stated honestly

Real: (1) the approval/honesty spine — trust is THE adoption blocker for agent products, and ours
is enforced by verify suites, not marketing; (2) the measured-outcome data flywheel; (3) vertical
depth compounding on one spine; (4) a cost structure near 90% gross margin (solo founder +
agent-maintained engineering). NOT a moat: model access, UI polish, feature count — funded
horizontals replicate those in quarters.

## Risks, named

Solo-founder bus factor (mitigation: everything is written down, verified, and agent-operable);
platform dependency on Anthropic/Supabase pricing; a funded leader pivoting into "run the
business" (mitigation: vertical depth + data they can't backfill); outbound compliance (CAN-SPAM/
TCPA/do-not-mail — already built conservatively, keep it that way); and the biggest one:
**distribution is unproven until Act I closes.** Everything above is conditional on that.
