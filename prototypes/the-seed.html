What to feel for

  • The page is an ordinary catalog. Scroll it, tap a piece — nothing new. Then rest your thumb on the dot and hold.
  • A small grid blooms under the thumb: one dot per piece on screen, same arrangement. You can't see it — your thumb covers it. You feel it. The page shows where you are.
  • Scrub the grid. One tick per piece. Park between two dots: it must not flicker.
  • Push past the bottom row and hold: the page turns under you, one row per tick. Pull back: it stops.
  • Rest on a piece and it turns to show its second view. Lift to open it. Fling and lift: nothing opens. Drag far off the dot: everything lets go.
  • The honest test: after a screenful, did your thumb move more than a coin's width?
Honest label: forty staged garments, drawn not photographed. What's real is the hold, the proxy mapping, the detents, the paging and the lift. The haptic detent exists only inside a native wrap — iPhone Safari has no web vibration (and the ringer switch silences the tick).
