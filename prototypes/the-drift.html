P14 · the drift — staged world, simulated steering

What to feel for

  • Scrub as before. To travel, rest your thumb in the ring at the bottom and push — any direction, any amount. The world flows, ticking past the needle near center. Release: it stops dead. You push, it never pushes.
  • The edges still work too: push into one with intent and hold. Brush one casually mid-scrub and the world must not move.
  • Dwell on what you like — the nebula of that kind glows brighter ahead. Nothing else changes: cells are placed, not sampled, and the label top-centre names the place you are in.
  • The glowing weather under the dots is real information: each nebula is a topic current. Read the land before you touch it.
  • Back-scrub your own path: the exact same dots, same positions, your fades intact. The walked world never rewrites.
  • Your route is drawn on the land. The Trail tab is the journey as an object — tap any stop to glide back to it.
  • Slow down and the dots around your selection bloom into labels — read three neighbours while feeling one. Skim and they vanish; they must never clutter speed.
  • Kinds feel different: a photo is a crisp click, a reel a rounder thud, a carousel a double tap. Full texture needs the Core Haptics plugin in the wrap; the stock tick only tells reels apart; on the web the sound carries it.
  • The map is a wheel. The middle is the mainstream; push out and it gets deeper — more extreme, more obscure, whatever this world's deep end is; each direction is a kind. HOME on the ring always points at the middle; DEEPER sits opposite. Push and the word you are heading for lights, with the kind ahead at the screen edge: you are telling the algorithm "deeper into horror", out loud.
  • Switch worlds (top-left chip, or W). New kinds, same wheel — your bearing and depth carry over. Read the kind names on the land before you touch it; the card names a place's register (serious, goofy) as a word, never as a colour.
Honest label: positions come from scores, not from learning — they cannot move today. Steering moves you; it is also logged as a preference, and in this demo the only thing that preference changes is the glow of the land ahead. Tomorrow's world is new. The staged content is a seeded simulation; the steering loop, the compass and the feel are real. A world loaded from worlds/*.json is an edition the pipeline wrote; the footer says which kind — YouTube (real shorts, YouTube's own player on open) or fixture (synthetic items, no player). Either way you control this app's algorithm, not YouTube's.
